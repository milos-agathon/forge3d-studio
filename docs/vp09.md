# VP-09 · Fly, camera view and camera keys

From the VP-09 worktree with its lockfile dependencies installed, run

```powershell
node scripts/vp09.mjs --engine-pythonpath C:/tmp/pr310-head/python
```

The path selects the local Forge3D 1.49.0 Python engine used for both native render lanes. Field Instrument needs its HDR writer and MapScene projection support. With that engine already on the interpreter's import path, the option can be omitted. The driver uses the VP-08 Windows WebView2 and Tauri setup, verifies the clean Field checkout at `64b7cf09` and checks the installed browser runtime against the vendored `d6459a26` inventory.

Each run prints a fresh evidence directory. `results.json` records the shell flags, observed backends, timed input, saved transforms, native camera payloads and per-landmark errors. The `field/` and `candidate/` directories contain corresponding viewport PNGs for the rolled pose, the moving take, both scrubbed keys and both played keys. They also contain native stills and exported keyframes. The candidate directory includes the 0° and 30° native renders and the rolled preview.

## Fly and camera capture

Field drew relief-only Bryce with WebGL2. Studio submitted WebGPU frames with `--disable-webgl`. Both drawing surfaces measured 794 × 727 physical pixels at DPR 1.

The replay looks 27 CSS pixels right and 16 pixels up, then holds W and E separately for 60 updates of 1/60 s. Both backends travelled 5 units on each axis, or 14 with Shift. S and Q reversed each movement. A 500 ms update while holding D moved 0.5 units. Release, blur, and exit followed by re-entry stopped held input. Vertical drags reached both pitch limits at ±1.55 radians. Exiting fly retained the drawn eye and heading with the orbit pivot 10 units ahead.

The free eye began at (9.5, 7, 11), looking at (0, 0.8, 0) from radius 15.801582. Studio entered the authored 30° roll with vertical FOV 53°, near 1.881489 and far 150.519102. End sent `setTransform` and `setActiveCamera` to `6b5d1927-e64a-4e48-93fe-62287c305035`. The saved eye was (11, 8, 13) and the saved ZYX Euler angles in XYZ component order were (5.323624°, 45.075855°, 40.703987°). The replay compares the saved rotation with the displayed quaternion. Numpad1 during flight captured the fly pose and kept fly active. Leaving camera view restored the free eye, quaternion, pivot, 45° FOV and 0.05–2000 clip range.

Field's first camera-entry frame levels the roll, and its exit retains the authored eye. The reference lane changes tools to make Field re-evaluate and draw the rolled camera before sampling. The driver reopens the same fixture in each app before the independent fly and recording measurements. Studio is checked directly on entry and exit.

## Timeline and Bryce landmarks

The replay records eight timeline frames at 24 fps with one controlled 42 ms update per frame. W is held throughout, with a nine-pixel look before frame five. The recorder writes six transform channels at frames 0, 4 and 8. The target camera owns the lens. Field flew at its free-view 45° FOV and played both keys at its authored 53° FOV. Studio used the target camera’s 53° FOV throughout the take, scrubbing and playback. The harness uses each application's own timeline and lens settings.

The replay projects Bryce’s four corners and peak from the cameras that drew them. Field uses a clone of its scene-draw camera. Studio uses the camera submitted with the WebGPU terrain samples. For Studio’s recording frames, the optical reference is Field’s scrubbed key. Both original take images are also kept. ## Input ownership

The candidate replay holds Shift before entering fly. W moves 14 units in 60 updates. In fly mode it types `east` in the Inspector Name field and presses End. It checks the text, the displayed pose and the native command log.

The rolled handoff runs in standalone WebGPU and in the editor-enabled WebView. From the 30° camera, W moves the eye five units. Escape restores the free 45° projection and 0.05–2000 clip range. Both views keep the flown eye, quaternion and ten-unit pivot on exit and for 30 idle updates. In standalone WebGPU the replay then drags the cube’s World X handle 22 CSS pixels. The cube moves 0.249585 Studio units along X. The submitted camera, including its up vector, matches the pre-drag frame exactly on movement and after release. The next wheel input resumes orbit.

In the editor-enabled WebView, the driver holds an orbit drag, enters camera view, moves the pointer 200 CSS pixels, leaves camera view and moves one more pixel before releasing. The restored eye stays fixed. In Rendered mode it snaps North one update after a wheel event and checks the displayed PNG against the completed request at native azimuth 270°. The bearing reads `BRG 000°`. The cube-only fixture checks the three positive axes and verifies that screen up in the top view points to world −Z.
